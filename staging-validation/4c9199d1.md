# Staging validation: memory-budget-update

## Change

The confidential-workflows enclave derives execution and compilation capacities
from a shared static memory budget. Settings are capped to that budget, and
reinjection reuses the compilation semaphore instead of multiplying permits.

## Validation steps

1. Roll the candidate to staging through the normal attestation and deployment
   process. Check the logged guest RAM, execution limit, and compilation limit;
   1992-2048 MiB guest RAM should yield one execution and one compilation.
2. Run representative confidential workflows and the real-Nitro capacity E2E.
   Burst beyond the execution limit, verify excess requests are rejected, and
   verify admitted workflows finish and subsequent requests succeed.
3. During load, reinject complete settings with unchanged or omitted compilation
   concurrency. Verify injection succeeds without adding compilation permits.
   A different effective concurrency must reject the payload without changing
   active settings; a fresh enclave must accept it within the memory-derived cap.
4. Check compilation queue wait, workflow latency, capacity rejections, enclave
   restarts, and memory use against the workload's staging baseline.
5. On x86_64, measure cold/warm compilation latency and peak/retained RSS with
   representative large WASM binaries at one and multiple compile slots.
   Measure execution and download memory separately before revising
   per-execution or compiler allowances.

Validation currently covers x86_64 only. ARM validation is deferred until its
platform blockers are resolved and hardware is available. The x86 results do
not validate ARM memory or timing behavior.

## Success criteria

Canaries and admitted workflows succeed, capacity is recovered after completion,
and reinjection does not increase concurrent compilation beyond the installed
limit. No OOMs or persistent enclave failures occur during the validation load.

## Edge cases and risks

The coefficients are estimates, not hard containment for arbitrary WASM. Small
enclaves have substantially less concurrency; guest RAM below 1892 MiB fails
startup. Monitor large binaries and cold-compilation bursts. Roll back for OOMs,
stuck admission, or unacceptable latency/rejection rates. Changing effective
compilation concurrency requires an enclave restart; unchanged credential
rotations remain supported.
